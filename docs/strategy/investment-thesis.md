# Investment thesis — preference authority

Status: hypothesis, not validated commercial traction. No paying customer, revenue, market-size or financial-impact claim is made.

## Problem and owned layer

An agent with memory can retrieve a preference and still misuse it: the person may reject this candidate, the dinner budget may apply only in this context, a proxy may not represent SELF, or one group member may veto. Operational permission to use a booking tool does not answer whether the subjective choice is justified. TastePolicy owns the explicit preference-authorization boundary, not the transaction.

Qloo supplies cultural relationships, entity identity and candidate evidence. TastePolicy adds subject-controlled state, contextual constraints, explicit-vs-inferred provenance, deterministic decisions, group decision rights, receipts and bounded continuation. Qloo is indispensable evidence, not a redundant component or a graph TastePolicy claims to own.

## First buyer and wedge

Initial buyer hypothesis: a small travel/concierge agent product team that already proposes hotels/places and needs inspectable user-specific pause/stop behavior before handing a choice to its separate execution/approval system. First wedge: one proposed place, five reviewed signals, a context-specific budget, an explicit veto and a traceable draft recommendation. No autonomous booking. Agentic commerce/personal shopping, hospitality, gifting and entertainment are adjacent hypotheses, not implemented markets or promised universal coverage.

Why now is a testable product premise: richer agent context and action interfaces make misuse of subjective preferences consequential. The existence of tool/agent protocols is not proof of customer demand. The next commercial proof is interviews and a bounded paid integration pilot, not another feature milestone.

What a buyer might pay for: an integration that reduces unjustified autonomous choices without excessive pauses, preserves decision evidence and makes user corrections reusable. Pricing hypothesis: bounded evaluation usage plus maintained state/receipt retention; no published pricing or unit economics are asserted. Provider licensing, cost, retention, support and data protection must be validated before selling production guarantees.

## Potential moat — not an existing monopoly

The compounding asset could be user-controlled preference histories with contextual applicability and explicit provenance, deterministic authorization, isolated subjects, truthful delegation/consent boundaries, group rights, receipts/continuations, reproducible TasteBench and integrations. Eventual receipt interoperability is future. M5 delegation remains user-attested, not verified external consent. Cultural intelligence itself belongs to Qloo. State must remain exportable/deletable; lock-in is not the privacy strategy.

## Evidence that would invalidate the thesis

- Buyers do not experience costly preference-boundary mistakes or will not pay to address them.
- A simple explicit-rule prompt with the same evidence performs equivalently and is easier to audit.
- Human evaluation shows unsafe autonomy or excessive ASK/UNAVAILABLE makes the integration unusable.
- Users will not maintain explicit state, or consent/subject separation is too burdensome.
- Provider coverage, cost, latency, licensing or availability makes the wedge uneconomic.
- Existing vendors satisfy the requirement with adequate provenance and lower integration cost.

No human pilot, competitive victory, commercial demand or investment readiness is claimed from green engineering tests alone. Guide owns competitive interpretation and Win Gate acceptance.

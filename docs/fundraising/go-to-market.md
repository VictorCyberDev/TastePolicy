# GTM and the 100-customer target

All counts below are targets. No current lead, pilot, contract or revenue is implied. Do not create CRM contacts or send outreach without the user's separate instructions.

## 30 / 60 / 90 days

Days 1–30: complete Qloo commercial terms and full onboarding; conduct 20 qualified buyer interviews; instrument signup-to-credential-to-first-real-decision with consent-respecting aggregate metrics; finish the two real assistant commissions and directory submissions. Target five design partners willing to name the integration problem. A discussion is not a customer.

Days 31–60: integrate five scoped pilots, record time to first decision and weekly retained use, compare outcomes with a simple rules/prompt baseline, and test the USD49 workspace price. Target three paying conversions after provider and billing gates. No charge is executed during sandbox acceptance. Publish two consenting customer case studies only if customers actually agree.

Days 61–90: improve the activation failure with the largest observed loss, grow to ten active design partners and target ten paying customers. Build repeatable docs/examples and directory discovery. Run one enterprise technical diligence exercise with explicit data scope. Continue only if the product delivers measurable value beyond existing rules.

The 100-paying-customer objective follows validated cohorts rather than an arbitrary 90-day claim. At assumed 20% qualified-trial-to-paid conversion it requires 500 qualified trials. At 30% signup-to-qualified-trial it requires about 1,667 signups. A further assumed 5% qualified-visitor-to-signup rate implies about 33,340 visitors. At 10% trial-to-paid, requirements double. These are funnel assumptions, not observed conversion rates or an acquisition forecast.

## Pipeline design

Stages: sourced account → problem qualified → technical owner confirmed → scoped trial agreed → first real decision → weekly retained use → commercial terms accepted → paid/active → renewed. Store stage entry UTC, named accountable owner, consent/source, use case, next action/date, blockers, expected contract value and confidence. Never count an unaccepted proposal as contracted ARR. Track lost reasons and stage aging.

Initial account profile: travel/concierge agent builders with a real candidate-selection flow and separate booking authorization. Expansion profiles: hospitality platform teams and commerce assistants with explicit preference problems. Avoid buying lists before the initial pain and conversion are validated. Directory traffic, open examples and integration partnerships are distribution hypotheses; no partner relationship is asserted.

## Metrics and experiment rules

Actual commercial metrics are currently unmeasured: qualified pipeline, activation, retention, paid conversion, MRR, churn, CAC, LTV, support hours and provider unit cost. Display null/unmeasured, never zero-filled simulated traction. Technical acceptance counts are separate from customer usage. Test users and synthetic benchmark requests must be excluded from adoption metrics.

For each cohort record eligibility, enrollment date, first credential, first real decision, repeat use, paid start and cancellation. Never label taste preferences, candidate names or credentials in analytics. ASK and rejection rates are diagnostic distributions with denominators, not automatic indicators of product quality. Publish a customer metric only after attribution and exclusions can be reproduced.

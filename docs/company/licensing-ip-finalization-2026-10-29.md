## Open-source versus hosted service

Apache-2.0 covers the repository code and separately licensed project assets. It does not grant:

- Qloo commercial API/output rights;
- rights to third-party trademarks or logos;
- rights to separately licensed media beyond their source licenses;
- a right to impersonate OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Qloo or other vendors;
- any certification or endorsement.

The hosted TastePolicy service may combine the open-source code with operator configuration, credentials, production infrastructure, external provider contracts and operational services that are not secrets or rights conveyed by cloning the repository.

## Contributor policy

Contributions are accepted under the repository's documented contribution policy and the license attached to submitted code. Contributors must have the right to submit their work and must not add credentials, proprietary third-party source, incompatible media or data obtained under terms that prohibit redistribution.

## Moat statement

TastePolicy's defensible implementation is not ownership of Qloo's graph. The moat is the authority layer built around cultural evidence: explicit user rules, action ceilings, scoped credentials, captured decisions, deterministic replay, leases/epochs, candidate/context commitments, portable proofs, revocation/status semantics, protocol interoperability and the accumulated conformance/benchmark corpus. Provider evidence and authority logic remain deliberately separable.
